# Definition

Retention strategies are the deliberate practices a business uses to reduce churn and increase expansion revenue from its existing customer base — spanning product (onboarding, feature adoption), customer success (proactive health monitoring), and revenue (pricing, packaging, renewal processes).

# Why it matters

Acquiring a new customer is almost always more expensive than retaining an existing one, and retention compounds: even a small improvement in monthly retention meaningfully increases average customer lifetime and therefore LTV. Because NRR and GRR are driven directly by retention execution, retention strategy is one of the highest-leverage areas for improving overall unit economics.

# Formula

Retention strategies don't have one formula, but their impact is measured through the metrics they influence:

```
Average Customer Lifetime (months) = 1 / Monthly Churn Rate
```

A small change in monthly churn has a large effect on lifetime:

| Monthly Churn | Average Lifetime |
|---|---|
| 5% | 20 months |
| 3% | ~33 months |
| 1% | 100 months |

# Example

A company identifies that customers who don't complete onboarding within the first 14 days churn at more than double the rate of those who do. By redesigning onboarding to drive faster time-to-value, the company reduces early-lifecycle churn — visible in cohort analysis as improved Month-1 and Month-2 retention for newer cohorts compared to older ones.

# Common mistakes

- Treating retention as solely a customer-success responsibility, ignoring that product usability and pricing/packaging also drive churn.
- Reacting to churn only after a cancellation request, instead of using leading indicators (declining usage, support tickets, failed payments) to intervene earlier.
- Applying the same retention playbook to every segment, when SMB and enterprise customers typically churn for very different reasons.
- Focusing exclusively on preventing churn while ignoring expansion — retention strategy should also actively grow revenue from healthy accounts, not just prevent losses.
- Not distinguishing voluntary churn (product/value problem) from involuntary churn (failed payments), which require completely different fixes.

# Related metrics

- **Churn Rate / GRR** — the direct metrics retention strategy aims to improve.
- **NRR** — retention strategy combined with expansion motions (upsell, cross-sell) drives NRR above 100%.
- **Cohort Analysis** — the standard method for measuring whether retention interventions are actually working over time.
- **LTV** — improved retention directly increases average customer lifetime and therefore LTV.
