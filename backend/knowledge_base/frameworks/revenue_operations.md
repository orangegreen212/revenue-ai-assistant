# Definition

Revenue Operations (RevOps) is the organizational function that aligns marketing, sales, and customer success around a single, shared view of the revenue process — common data definitions, a unified tech stack, and consistent reporting — instead of each team operating with its own tools, metrics, and definitions.

# Why it matters

Without RevOps, marketing, sales, and CS often define "customer," "opportunity," or "churn" differently, produce conflicting reports, and hand off leads/accounts through broken processes. RevOps exists to remove these seams: one CRM data model, one definition of each metric, and one team responsible for the health of the full revenue funnel from lead to renewal.

# Formula

RevOps is a function, not a single calculated metric, but it is typically measured through the health of the metrics it governs across the funnel:

```
Funnel efficiency = (Opportunities × Win Rate × Average Deal Size) / Sales Cycle Length
```

A RevOps team is also commonly evaluated on whether the following stay consistent across systems:

| Check | What it verifies |
|---|---|
| Single source of truth | Marketing, Sales, and CS report the same MRR/ARR figure |
| Consistent stage definitions | "Opportunity," "Customer," "Churned" mean the same thing everywhere |
| End-to-end funnel visibility | Lead → MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Customer → Renewal is trackable in one place |

# Example

A company's marketing team reports 500 MQLs this month, sales reports 40 new customers, and CS reports 35 renewals — but marketing counts a "customer" the moment a contract is signed, while finance counts it once the first invoice is paid. RevOps resolves this by defining a single "customer" event that all three teams reference, so every team's dashboard reconciles to the same number.

# Common mistakes

- Treating RevOps as purely a tooling/CRM-administration role rather than a strategic function that owns end-to-end funnel definitions.
- Allowing each department to keep its own definition of core terms ("customer," "active," "churned") instead of centralizing them.
- Building RevOps reporting only for sales, ignoring how marketing and customer success data feed into the same funnel.
- Measuring RevOps success by activity (dashboards built, tickets closed) instead of by whether reported numbers are actually consistent across teams.

# Related metrics

- **Sales Funnel** — the end-to-end process RevOps is responsible for instrumenting.
- **Customer Segmentation** — RevOps typically owns the shared definitions used to segment accounts consistently across teams.
- **NRR / Churn** — cross-functional metrics that require marketing, sales, and CS data to agree in order to be trustworthy.
