# Definition

Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) — sometimes ARPA (Average Revenue Per Account) in B2B contexts — is the average recurring revenue generated per customer, account, or active user over a given period. It is a monetization-intensity metric, not a growth metric.

# Why it matters

ARPU shows how effectively a business monetizes its customer base, independent of how many customers it has. Rising ARPU (from pricing changes, plan mix shifts, or expansion) and rising customer count are two separate growth levers — separating them is essential for understanding *why* revenue is changing.

# Formula

```
ARPU = Total Recurring Revenue (MRR or ARR) / Number of Active Customers
```

Seat-based variant for per-seat pricing:

```
ARPU (per seat) = Total MRR / Number of Paid Seats
```

| Period | Formula |
|---|---|
| Monthly ARPU | MRR / Active Customers |
| Annual ARPU | ARR / Active Customers |

# Example

A company has $50,000 MRR across 250 active customers.

```
ARPU = $50,000 / 250 = $200 per customer per month
```

# Common mistakes

- Mixing "customers," "accounts," and "seats" in the denominator without a clear, consistent definition of what a "user" means for the business.
- Including one-time fees in the ARPU numerator, inflating the figure beyond what recurring revenue alone would show.
- Comparing ARPU across companies with different packaging models (per-seat vs. flat-fee-per-account) as if they were equivalent.
- Using an ARPU snapshot at period-end when the customer base changed significantly mid-period, without reconciling for that movement.

# Related metrics

- **MRR / ARR** — the numerator in the ARPU calculation.
- **LTV** — ARPU is a direct input to the standard LTV formula.
- **Customer Segmentation** — blended ARPU can hide very different monetization patterns across SMB, mid-market, and enterprise segments.
- **CAC Payback** — ARPU (combined with gross margin) determines how quickly acquisition cost is recovered.
